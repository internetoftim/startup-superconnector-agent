**1. Demand reality — who has this problem and how badly?**
Startup super-connectors (SSCs): ecosystem builders whose value is their network.
Primary evidence — discovery interview last night with one (a startup event
organiser) recruiting ~200 investors: network fragmented across SMS/WhatsApp/
LinkedIn, time drained "replying to the same people every day" instead of making
new connections. I'm an SSC myself — Angel Squad, VC scout, AI Hive, 30+ events.
The scarce resource is the connector's own attention.

**2. Status quo — what do they do today?**
Manual DMs across five apps + a CRM/spreadsheet + memory. The "AI" tools that
exist automate OUTBOUND (SDR blasters) or RSVP forms — they act on humans. Nobody
gives the connector a PROXY that transacts with other people's agents.

**3. Desperate specificity — the narrowest painful moment?**
A founder asks for an intro to the right angel who can meet in person next month.
Doing it well = check who fits, confirm both sides want it, protect private info,
find a mutual time — 6 DMs across 3 apps over a week. Multiply by a dozen live
asks and the connector never gets to NEW relationships.

**4. Narrowest wedge — smallest thing worth building?**
One brokered outcome across THREE agents: a founder's agent asks my matchmaker
agent for a thesis-fit investor; the matchmaker curates + enforces both sides'
consent; the investor's agent filters and accepts; -> a signed tri-party intro.
Founders and investors are first-class users with their own agents; the
matchmaker is the trusted hub between them. Not a CRM. Not an SDR. Logistics is
a follow-on shown only if time.

**5. Observation — what do I see that others don't?**
As founders and investors get their own agents, those agents STILL can't just
cold-call each other — no trust, no curation, no privacy. Someone has to broker,
and the broker is exactly the connector both sides already trust. A2A v1.0
(Linux Foundation, 150+ orgs, native in Azure/Bedrock/GCP) makes agent-to-agent
dealflow possible; the trusted matchmaker is what makes it SAFE. Everyone's
building agents that DO tasks or represent ONE side; the gap is the trusted
agent in the MIDDLE.

**6. Future-fit — why now?**
A2A standardised cross-org agent communication this year. The proxy-to-proxy
pattern generalises: today an intro, tomorrow event slots, advisory time, deal
flow — anywhere a person's interests must be reconciled with another's. The
super-connector is the sharpest wedge because their whole job IS reconciliation.

## Premise challenges to expect
- "Isn't this an SDR / outbound bot?" No — those act on humans and push one
  agenda. This reconciles TWO principals' interests and enforces BOTH sides'
  guardrails. The +consent/escalation behaviour is the point.
- "Isn't this just a CRM with AI?" A CRM stores the network; it doesn't
  NEGOTIATE on your behalf with another person's agent. The transaction is the
  product.
- "Won't founders' and investors' agents just skip the matchmaker?" No — neither
  trusts a stranger's agent on fit, privacy, or vouching; the matchmaker is the
  party both already trust, and dual-consent + bounded vouching are its
  irreducible value. A2A makes the connection possible; trust makes it usable.
- "Chicken-and-egg — does the other side have an agent?" Demo shows three agents;
  in the interim the same proxy drafts for human channels (degrade gracefully).
  A2A adoption is the tailwind; we get more magical as it lands.
- "n=1?" Signal + my own SSC experience across 30+ events; grabbing more quotes
  from organisers at today's breaks.
- "Trust — would you let an agent commit for you?" That's why autonomy limits +
  escalation are core, not bolted on: the agent commits ONLY inside mandate and
  escalates the rest. Demo shows a real escalation.

## Do NOT let office-hours scope-creep you
CRM, payments, multi-party graph optimisation, full event platform = "future."
Hold the wedge: two proxies, one brokered intro, signed agreement. Beat B only
if Beat A is bulletproof.
