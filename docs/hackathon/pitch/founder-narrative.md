# Founder Narrative & Judge Q&A — Superconnector

## Your 20-second founder story
"I'm a super-connector — Angel Squad, VC scout, AI Hive, 30+ AI events. My job is
brokering between founders and investors, and it drowns you in DMs across five
apps. Now those founders and investors are getting their own agents — but their
agents can't cold-call each other; someone trusted has to broker. I built that
matchmaker as an agent: founders' and investors' agents negotiate THROUGH it, it
enforces both sides' consent and vouches within limits. Same engine, different
profile per role. I contribute to AG2; I built my own."

## Judge Q&A — anticipated pokes

**"Once founders and investors have their own agents, why not let them talk directly — why a matchmaker?"**
Because neither side trusts a stranger's agent to be honest about fit, to protect
their private context (a founder's deck, an investor's availability and thesis),
or to vouch. The matchmaker is the party both already trust. A2A makes agent-to-
agent dealflow *possible*; the trusted matchmaker makes it *safe*. Curation +
dual-consent enforcement + bounded vouching are the irreducible value in the
middle — and they compound into a network/reputation moat a raw directory can't
retrofit.

**"Isn't this just an SDR / outbound automation bot?"**
Those act ON humans and push one agenda. This reconciles TWO principals'
interests and enforces BOTH sides' guardrails — it refuses to share a founder's
deck without consent, refuses to surface a contact who hasn't opted in, and
escalates anything above its authority. Representation, not blasting.

**"Isn't this a CRM with AI on top?"**
A CRM stores your network; it doesn't NEGOTIATE for you with someone else's
agent. The transaction between two representatives is the product; the profile is
just its memory.

**"Chicken-and-egg — does the counterparty have an agent?"**
A2A v1.0 (Linux Foundation, 150+ orgs, native in Azure/Bedrock/GCP) is the
tailwind — proxy agents are arriving. Until they do, the same agent degrades
gracefully to drafting for human channels. We're useful now and more magical as
adoption lands.

**"Would you actually let an agent commit on your behalf?"**
Only inside an explicit mandate. The profile encodes what the agent may commit
autonomously (opted-in intros, pre-cleared calendar windows) vs must escalate
(anything financial, sharing private info, endorsements). The demo shows a real
escalation — that boundary is the product, not an afterthought.

**"n=1 interview isn't a market."**
Signal, not proof — stacked on my own SSC experience across 30+ events. Grabbing
more connector quotes at today's breaks.

**"Why does representation generalise?"**
Because the primitive is portable: the same profile that brokers an intro brokers
an event slot, an advisory hour, a piece of deal flow. Any two-sided
interest-reconciliation is the same shape.

**"Why you?"**
I'm the user (SSC, 30+ events), the framework contributor (AG2), and an
enterprise platform operator (EY, Graphcore). Building my own tool on the
standard I already work in.
